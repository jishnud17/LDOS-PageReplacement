BACKGROUND
----------
Three synthetic workloads were run on CloudLab c220g2 (Xeon E5-2660 v3, Haswell,
Ubuntu 22.04, kernel 5.15).  PEBS sampled every 5003 retired memory uops; UFFD
tracked write-protect faults.  Per-page signals were computed in rolling 64-bar
windows of 50 ms each and exported to CSV.  The reactivity script then detected
state-change events (cold_to_hot, hot_to_cold, migration) per page, aligned all
instances at t=0, and scored each signal by:

  amplitude   -- how far the signal moves around the event (z-score units)
  consistency -- fraction of individual events that move in the same direction
  lead_lag_ms -- when the move starts relative to the transition (negative = LEADS)
  score       -- amplitude * consistency  (the primary ranking metric)

Three workloads produced complementary data:
  hot_cold   -- 10% hot (64 hits/sweep), 20% warm (8), 70% cold (1/37 pages)
  sequential -- uniform 4 hits/page/sweep across all pages
  temporal   -- phase 1: region A hot (3 s), phase 2: region B hot (3 s), A goes cold

================================================================================
SIGNAL GROUPS -- WHICH SIGNALS MEASURE THE SAME THING
================================================================================

Understanding the overlap is critical before building the phase-2 model.
Keeping redundant signals wastes model capacity and can mislead importance ranking.

GROUP 1: VOLATILITY / MAGNITUDE-OF-RATE-CHANGE  [rwi_high, sqn, si, asi, rwi_low]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All five measure the same underlying concept: how large a move the access rate
just made relative to its recent baseline.

  rwi_high  -- Random Walk Index (high period).  Compares actual range to the
                range expected from a random walk.  rwi > 1 means directional move.
  sqn       -- System Quality Number.  Expectancy / std of rate changes.  Measures
                trend quality; spikes when the rate moves monotonically.
  si        -- Swing Index.  Uses |close - prev_close| relative to True Range.
                Numerically different from rwi/sqn but fires on the same events.
  asi       -- Accumulation Swing Index.  Cumulative sum of SI.  Because each bar
                adds to the running total, ASI is just SI with memory.
  rwi_low   -- RWI computed over the low period (shorter lookback).  Consistently
                weaker than rwi_high (~60-80% of its score) because the shorter
                window catches less of the sustained move.

  >>> rwi_high and sqn are nearly interchangeable.  si and asi are nearly
      interchangeable (asi = cumulative si).  Pick ONE from this group.
      rwi_high is recommended: highest score, most consistent across workloads.

GROUP 2: TREND/CYCLE STATE  [stc, stc_signal]
----------------------------------------------
These two are the same indicator at different smoothing stages.

  stc        -- Schaff Trend Cycle raw output.
  stc_signal -- Signal line of STC (another EMA pass over stc).

  Both consistently LAG transitions by +50 to +100 ms (they confirm after the
  fact, they do not predict).  They are useful as confirmation signals, not
  as features for early prediction.

  >>> stc and stc_signal are redundant; stc_signal adds one more EMA lag on top
      of stc.  If you include this group, use stc only.

GROUP 3: BINARY DIRECTION FLAGS  [psar_dir, supertrend_dir]
------------------------------------------------------------
Both output +1 (uptrend) or -1 (downtrend).

  psar_dir       -- direction flip of the Parabolic SAR.
  supertrend_dir -- direction flip of the Supertrend band.

  Both react at hot->cold transitions (score ~0.40-1.11) and are coincident
  (lead_lag ~0 ms).  supertrend_dir is consistently weaker than psar_dir.

  >>> Keep psar_dir, drop supertrend_dir.

GROUP 4: DIRECTIONAL MOVEMENT  [plus_di, minus_di, adx]
---------------------------------------------------------
All three come from the same Directional Movement calculation.

  plus_di   -- positive DI: upward directional momentum.
  minus_di  -- negative DI: downward directional momentum.
  adx       -- average of |+DI - -DI| / (+DI + -DI) over the lookback period.

  minus_di is the most useful of the three for hot->cold detection (score
  0.40-1.43 across workloads).  adx and plus_di are weaker and noisier.

  >>> If you need a direction signal from this family, use minus_di only.

GROUP 5: AROON FAMILY  [aroon_up, aroon_down, aroon_osc]
---------------------------------------------------------
aroon_osc = aroon_up - aroon_down.  It is algebraically derived from the other
two, so all three carry identical information.

  aroon_up   -- time since the highest rate in the lookback window.
  aroon_down -- time since the lowest rate in the lookback window.
  aroon_osc  -- their difference.

  Moderate reactivity (score ~0.10-2.40 on temporal).  aroon_osc is the most
  compact summary of the three.

  >>> Keep aroon_osc, drop aroon_up and aroon_down.

GROUP 6: INTER-ACCESS TIMING  [inter_access_interval_ms, inter_access_variance_ms2]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both are derived from the distribution of gaps between consecutive accesses.

  inter_access_interval_ms   -- mean gap between accesses in the bar window.
  inter_access_variance_ms2  -- variance of those gaps.

  inter_access_interval_ms is the ONLY signal that reliably LEADS cold_to_hot:
  lead_lag = -100 ms to -250 ms across workloads.  Before a page heats up,
  access gaps begin tightening -- the interval signal catches this early.
  Consistency is moderate (0.53-0.72) so it is noisier than rwi/sqn, but its
  predictive lead makes it uniquely valuable for the phase-2 model.

  inter_access_variance_ms2 is a weaker, nosier companion.  It occasionally
  leads cold_to_hot but with lower consistency.

  >>> Keep inter_access_interval_ms.  inter_access_variance_ms2 is optional
      (adds noise, minor incremental signal over the mean).

GROUP 7: ICHIMOKU CLOUD  [ich_tenkan, ich_kijun, ich_senkou_a, ich_senkou_b, ich_chikou]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All five are moving-average-type smoothings of the access rate over different
lookback windows (9, 26, 52 bars).  They are far too slow to respond to the
transitions in this data (6-second workloads with 50 ms bars).

  Additionally: ich_chikou produces IDENTICAL output to interval_access_rate
  in every workload (same amplitude, same score, same lead_lag).  This is a
  code-level issue -- the chikou line appears to be replicated from the raw rate.

  Scores across all workloads and events: amplitude < 0.42, score < 0.40.
  In most configurations they are essentially noise (score < 0.05).

  >>> DROP ENTIRE GROUP.  No predictive or reactive value in this dataset.

GROUP 8: LINEAR REGRESSION  [linreg_slope, linreg_intercept]
-------------------------------------------------------------
Both are near-zero across all workloads and events (score < 0.03 in most cases).
The regression window is too long for 50 ms transitions.

  >>> DROP BOTH.  Not reactive to state changes at this timescale.

GROUP 9: VHF AND GAPO  [vhf, gapo]
------------------------------------
Both measure the ratio of net price movement to total path length (trend vs.
oscillation detection).

  vhf   -- Vertical Horizontal Filter.  Moderate reactivity (score 0.37-0.58 on
            hot_to_cold), consistent (~0.80), coincident.
  gapo  -- Gap Oscillator.  Slightly weaker than vhf in most workloads.

  These measure the same concept from slightly different angles.  vhf is the
  stronger performer.

  >>> Keep vhf, drop gapo if reducing feature count.

GROUP 10: MISC INDIVIDUAL SIGNALS
-----------------------------------
  trix       -- Triple-smoothed rate of change.  HIGH amplitude (up to 5.6 on
                temporal cold_to_hot) but extremely LOW consistency (0.06-0.18).
                Fires loudly but randomly.  Not reliable.  DROP.

  ravi       -- Relative vigor; moderate reactivity.  Lags transitions.
                Optional, no unique predictive contribution.

  heat_score -- Composite of UFFD + PEBS counts.  Moderate reactivity (score
                0.10-1.57), coincident.  Useful as a secondary confirmation
                feature but does not lead transitions.

  recency_weighted_freq -- Near-zero reactivity across all workloads (score < 0.01).
                           DROP.

  access_rate / interval_access_rate -- Raw UFFD-based rate.  Near-zero reactivity
                (score < 0.007).  PEBS-based signals outperform this by orders of
                magnitude.  DROP as standalone features; they are already embedded
                in the signal computations.

  psar       -- The raw Parabolic SAR value (not direction).  Negligible reactivity.
                Use psar_dir instead.

  supertrend -- Raw Supertrend band value.  Negligible reactivity.  Use
                psar_dir or supertrend_dir instead (and prefer psar_dir).

SUMMARY: RECOMMENDED FEATURE SET FOR PHASE-2 MODEL
================================================================================

Based on cross-workload consistency, lead timing, and inter-signal redundancy:

  KEEP (non-redundant, reactive, at least partially leading):

    rwi_high                 -- Top volatility signal.  Coincident.  Highest and
                                most consistent score in all 3 workloads.
                                Represents the entire GROUP 1 family.

    stc                      -- Trend/cycle state.  Lags +50-100 ms but has high
                                consistency.  The only signal in its group with
                                meaningful reactivity.  Use as confirmation feature.

    inter_access_interval_ms -- ONLY signal that leads cold_to_hot (-100 to -250 ms).
                                Noisier than rwi/sqn but provides timing information
                                no other signal has.  Critical for the prediction task.

    psar_dir                 -- Binary direction flag.  Coincident.  Low-cost feature
                                for the model to learn transition direction.

    minus_di                 -- Directional indicator.  Hot_to_cold detection.
                                Complements psar_dir with a continuous value.

    vhf                      -- Trend-vs-oscillation filter.  Moderate reactivity,
                                high consistency.  Different axis from rwi_high.

  OPTIONAL (adds marginal signal, not redundant):

    heat_score               -- Composite UFFD+PEBS count.  Non-zero contribution
                                in sequential workload.  Low effort to include.

    aroon_osc                -- Time-since-high/low.  Moderate reactivity.

  DROP (redundant or non-reactive):

    sqn, si, asi, rwi_low    -- Same information as rwi_high.
    stc_signal               -- Same as stc with extra lag.

================================================================================
HONEST CAVEATS (for mentor discussion)
================================================================================

1. SYNTHETIC WORKLOADS ONLY
   All three workloads were purpose-built to produce graded intensity and clean
   transitions.  Real application memory patterns are messier and may surface
   different signal rankings.  This is phase-1 discovery; phase-2 should validate
   on a real workload trace (e.g., Redis, SPEC, or a research benchmark).

2. RESIDUAL PEBS THROTTLE DIPS
   At sample period 5003, the kernel still produced occasional throttle bursts on
   heavily-hammered hot pages.  These cause 1-2 bar dips in access_rate that are
   visible in raw data.  The median smoothing in the event detector suppresses
   false event detection from these dips, but they add low-level noise to signal
   values computed over bars containing dips.  This is cosmetic: hot pages never
   reach zero rate during the active phase.

3. SINGLE RUN PER WORKLOAD
   Each workload was run once.  Signal rankings are stable enough to present as
   findings, but replication (2-3 runs, compare ranks) would strengthen the case.
   The top-3 ranking (rwi_high/sqn/si) is unlikely to change; lower-ranked signals
   may swap positions across runs.

4. GROUP 1 IS NEAR-DEGENERATE
   rwi_high, sqn, and si all measure the same thing (magnitude of rate change).
   Their high scores reflect the fact that rate changes ARE the transitions --
   these signals are reactive by construction.  The interesting finding is whether
   any signal leads the transition, which is where inter_access_interval_ms stands
   out as genuinely predictive rather than merely reactive.

5. MIGRATION EVENT RELIABILITY
   Migration event counts are small (92-267 per workload) compared to cold_to_hot
   and hot_to_cold (1000-9000+).  Consistency scores of 1.0 on migration events
   should be treated skeptically -- small n inflates consistency estimates.
