One ranking, computed over EVERY dataset in the study (this supersedes the
earlier three-ranking split by evidence source).

HOW THE RANKING IS BUILT
------------------------
Per dataset and event type the analysis produces, for each signal:
    amplitude   how far it moves around the transition (std-dev units)
    consistency fraction of individual events moving the SAME direction
    lead_lag_ms when it moves relative to t=0 (negative = leads)
    score       amplitude x consistency

Those 25 ranking tables (12 datasets x their event types) are then combined
with four stated rules:

  1. EXCLUDE NON-INDICATORS.  interval_access_rate (and its code-duplicate
     ichimoku_chikou) IS the access rate -- the series events are defined
     from.  It tops nearly every table by construction, which is circular,
     so it is excluded from indicator ranking.  A phase-2 model may still
     use the raw rate as an input.
  2. CONSISTENCY FLOOR ~0.6.  Below that a signal points the wrong way too
     often for amplitude to redeem it.
  3. BREADTH OVER PEAK.  Placing top-5 in many tables beats a huge score in
     one.  "top3"/"top5" below count tables out of 25.
  4. REDUNDANCY PENALTY.  Signals carrying the same information as a
     stronger sibling are demoted regardless of score -- the output is a
     feature set, not a leaderboard.

Evidence base: 12 datasets -- 3 synthetic (UFFD), 1 measurement control
(same synthetic under PEBS), and 8 real-workload runs (GUPS x4 replicated,
GAPBS PageRank, GAPBS BC, XSBench, liblinear).

------------------------------------------------------------------- TIER 1 ----
CORE FEATURES -- strong and consistent across nearly every dataset
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 1. RANDOM WALK INDEX (HIGH)      random_walk_index_high
    Compares how far the rate actually travelled over the window against
    how far a random walk of the same volatility would.  High = real
    directional move, not jitter.
    EVIDENCE: top-3 in 21 of 25 tables, top-5 in 22.  Median consistency
    0.98, median score 1.80 -- the highest of any indicator.  #1 on
    synthetic (UFFD), on the PEBS control, and on real workloads spanning
    graph sweeps (PageRank 2.63/0.98), HPC physics (XSBench 2.50/0.97) and
    ML training (liblinear 4.79/0.96, and 2.68 at a perfect 1.00).
    The single unconditional keeper in the study.

 2. SWING INDEX                   swing_index
    Size of the current bar's move scaled by the bar's range -- a
    normalised first derivative.  Reacts within one bar, no window to fill.
    EVIDENCE: top-3 in 15 of 25 tables, top-5 in 21.  Median score 1.22 at
    0.87 consistency.  Complements #1: instantaneous step size vs windowed
    path.  Keep both; they are not redundant.

 3. RANDOM WALK INDEX (LOW)       random_walk_index_low
    Same statistic over a shorter lookback.
    EVIDENCE: top-3 in 11 of 25, top-5 in 15, median score 1.34 at 0.91.
    Peaked at 3.59/3.57 with PERFECT 1.00 consistency on both replicate
    runs of gups-skewed.  The short window catches sharp demotions the
    long window smooths over -- worth carrying alongside #1.

------------------------------------------------------------------- TIER 2 ----
SITUATIONAL -- real signal, but conditional on workload or role
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 4. SCHAFF TREND CYCLE            schaff_trend_cycle
    Cycle detector on double-smoothed moving averages; needs several bars
    to confirm.  Top-5 in 8 of 25, median score 0.69 at 0.82 consistency.
    LAGS in EVERY dataset without exception (+1 to +2 bars).  Its role is
    fixed: confirmation that a transition really happened, never prediction.

 5. SYSTEM QUALITY NUMBER         system_quality_number
    Mean rate-change over the std of rate-changes.  Top-5 in 9 of 25,
    median 0.92 consistency, median score 0.81.
    *** CORRECTION TO EARLIER CONCLUSIONS ***  This signal was previously
    dropped as a "PEBS quantization artifact", on the strength of a single
    comparison: it fell from 2.34 on hot_cold under UFFD to 0.18 on the
    same workload under PEBS.  The wider dataset does NOT support
    generalising that.  Under PEBS it scores 1.45/1.36 at 1.00 consistency
    on both gups-skewed replicates, 1.34 on PageRank, ~0.99 on XSBench and
    liblinear.  The collapse is specific to hot_cold, not to PEBS.  Correct
    verdict: VARIABLE, not artifactual -- genuinely strong on some
    workloads, weak on others (gups-hotset-move 0.54-0.90 consistency).
    Reinstated as situational rather than dropped.

 6. RECENCY-WEIGHTED FREQUENCY    recency_weighted_frequency
    Access rate with exponential decay favouring recent bars.  Top-5 in 8
    of 25 at a very high 0.98 median consistency -- but median amplitude
    only 0.14.
    Sharply split by measurement: essentially dead under UFFD (0.00-0.09)
    and useful under PEBS (0.95-2.18 at 0.98-1.00), which fits -- it is a
    smoother, and PEBS-sampled rates are the noisy ones.  Always LAGS.
    A reliable confirmation input on sampled telemetry; redundant with the
    raw rate if the rate is already a feature.

 7. PARABOLIC SAR DIRECTION       parabolic_sar_direction
    +1/-1 flag for which side of a trailing trend level the rate sits on.
    Top-5 in 5 of 25, but median consistency 0.67 -- close to the floor.
    Cheap categorical direction input; never top-tier.

 8. INTER-ACCESS INTERVAL         inter_access_interval_ms
    Mean time gap between consecutive accesses, from hardware PEBS
    timestamps.  THE ONLY SIGNAL IN THE STUDY THAT HAS EVER LED A
    TRANSITION -- and that remains its entire case.
    Be precise about how narrow the evidence is: median score across all
    25 tables is 0.08, near the bottom.  It is flat on most workloads.  It
    spikes on exactly two: the PEBS control (4.29 sigma, -449ms lead) and
    the synthetic temporal handoff (6.04 sigma, -100ms) -- both workloads
    where pages warm up GRADUALLY.  On step-transition workloads (GUPS
    relocates its hotset instantaneously) it is ~0.00 -- but so is every
    other signal's lead there, because a step function has no precursor to
    detect.  XSBench and liblinear likewise have no warm-up ramps.
    VERDICT: keep, but as a conditional feature whose value depends on the
    workload having gradual transitions -- not as a general-purpose signal.
    Confirming it needs a warm-up-shaped real workload (memcached/redis
    cache fill would be the natural test).

================================================================================
CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

1. RANDOM WALK INDEX IS THE RESULT.  Top-3 in 21 of 25 tables at 0.98
   median consistency, holding across two measurement methods (UFFD and
   PEBS), four hardware platforms, and five workload classes -- synthetic
   patterns, random RMW updates, graph sweeps, HPC Monte Carlo, and ML
   training.  Nothing else in the study is close to that breadth.

2. SWING INDEX IS THE COMPLEMENT.  Second on breadth, and it measures a
   different thing (single-bar step size vs windowed path), so the pair
   covers both fast and sustained movement.

3. LAGGING SIGNALS ARE STILL USEFUL, JUST NOT PREDICTIVE.  Schaff Trend
   Cycle lags in every single dataset -- that consistency makes it a good
   confirmation input and a bad forecaster.  Same for
   recency_weighted_frequency.

4. THE PREDICTIVE CLAIM IS REAL BUT NARROW.  inter_access_interval_ms is
   the only signal that has ever moved BEFORE a transition (-449ms at 4.3
   sigma).  It does so only where transitions ramp gradually; on
   step-transition workloads nothing leads, itself included.  This is a
   statement about workloads, not a defect in the signal -- but the
   supporting evidence is 2 datasets out of 12 and should be described
   that way.

5. TWO EARLIER VERDICTS DID NOT SURVIVE THE FULL DATASET.
   - system_quality_number was called a PEBS artifact on one workload
     comparison; it is fine on most PEBS datasets and is better described
     as variable.  Corrected above.
   - ADX and linear regression were briefly top-ranked on a run whose
     sampling clock had stretched ~30x; at measured cadence both collapse.
     That verdict held.

6. MEASUREMENT CAVEATS THAT BOUND ALL OF THE ABOVE.
   - Bar cadence ran 1.8x-7.1x nominal on the real workloads (measured per
     dataset by the analysis and reported in each run).  Sufficient to
     separate fast from slow signals -- demonstrated by the smoothers
     collapsing -- but lead/lag values are quantized to the measured bar.
   - Large negative lead values equal to the window edge (-929, -1141,
     -1785 ms) mean "no clean onset found", not "predicts that far ahead".
   - Haswell-class PEBS attributes stores only; Ice Lake attributes both.
   - gapbs cc_sv/kron is not measurable by this method at all (median
     per-page rate 0.0 -- activity too thinly spread for per-page bars).
