HOTNESS CHANNEL COMPARISON -- rate vs write-touch, scored against ground truth
==============================================================================
r650 (Xeon Platinum 8360Y, Ice Lake), GUPS hot-set relocation, 2026-08-03
Datasets: data/real_workloads/touch10_gups_move_{c50,c100,c200,c400}

THE QUESTION
  Phase-1 rankings label hot/cold transitions from interval_access_rate --
  the same quantity most of the ranked signals derive from.  Is there a
  hotness channel that does NOT depend on access rate, and which channel
  actually agrees with what the program did?

GROUND TRUTH (no measurement involved)
  Patched gups-hotset-move logs its relocation timestamp and geometry.  Its
  own arithmetic (index1 += hotsize for index1 < hotsize/4) partitions each
  thread's slice:
      [0, 32K)      32 pages  hot -> cold   POSITIVES
      [32K, 128K)   96 pages  stay hot      CONTROLS
      [128K, 160K)  32 pages  cold -> hot
  The 96 controls are what makes this decisive: same signal regime, same
  tracking history, unaffected by the move.  A detector that fires on them
  is producing false positives among pages it cannot distinguish from the
  true events.

RESULT
                     RATE CHANNEL              WRITE-TOUCH CHANNEL
  bar        recall   false   latency     recall   false   latency
  151 ms         9%    100%     -32 ms      100%      0%    116 ms
  304 ms        56%    100%    -562 ms      100%      0%    317 ms
  600 ms        69%    100%    -361 ms      100%      0%    811 ms
  1213 ms       88%    100%    1771 ms      100%      0%   1771 ms

  recall  = of the 32 truly relocated pages, % flagged at the move
  false   = of the 96 pages that stayed hot, % flagged anyway
  latency = median delay from the program's relocation to detection

READING IT
  The rate channel flags EVERY control page at every cadence.  It cannot
  separate pages the program stopped touching from pages it kept touching,
  so its "detections" carry no information regardless of recall -- and the
  negative latencies at 151-600 ms bars are the signature of a detector
  firing on a measurement artifact rather than on the event.

  The write-touch channel is exact at every cadence: all 32 positives, zero
  false alarms on the 96 controls.  Its detection latency tracks bar width
  (0.8-1.5 bars) because that is the sampling floor -- a per-bar channel
  cannot resolve a transition faster than one bar.

WHY THE RATE CHANNEL FAILS HERE
  Not circularity alone.  Per-page PEBS crediting on this platform is
  instruction-mix dependent: pages of identical true heat drew 0.2% vs
  20.7% of samples depending on which branch the workload was executing
  (see analyze_oor.sh, probe_regime.sh, and the commits of 2026-08-03).
  Capture volume, workload throughput, and the manager's own accounting
  were all verified identical across the two regimes, so the effect sits
  below the kernel interface.  Rate labels are therefore not merely
  self-referential -- they are wrong for cache-resident pages.

THE CHANNEL
  Soft-dirty PTE bits, 10 ms windows (LDOS_TOUCH_CHANNEL=1,
  LDOS_TOUCH_WINDOW_CYCLES=1), exported as touch_windows.  Per bar the
  delta is "how many 10 ms windows saw a write" -- no sampling, no event
  selection, no DataLA attribution.

COSTS AND LIMITS (all measured, none hidden)
  - ~6.4x workload slowdown: clear_refs resets soft-dirty process-wide and
    write-protects every resident PTE, so short windows are expensive.  The
    labels therefore describe a workload the instrument itself slowed.
  - Window length is not free: at 200 ms the channel SATURATES (relocated
    and unmoved pages both read 1.00) because GUPS's 10% uniform traffic
    keeps every page warm.  10 ms gives 0.17.  Sized by probe_touch_window.sh.
  - WRITES only.  Fine for GUPS (read-modify-write); a read-hot page is
    invisible to this channel.
  - Ground truth exists only for the instrumented synthetic workload.
    Anything claimed about XSBench/GAPBS/liblinear transfers by assumption.
  - The latency channel (PERF_SAMPLE_WEIGHT) reads zero on this node and is
    excluded.  It would have inherited the same crediting artifact anyway,
    being derived from the same per-page attribution.
