BACKGROUND
----------
Three synthetic workloads were run on CloudLab c220g2 (Xeon E5-2660 v3, Haswell,
Ubuntu 22.04, kernel 5.15).  PEBS sampled every 5003 retired memory uops; UFFD
tracked write-protect faults.  Per-page signals were computed in rolling 64-bar
windows of 50 ms each and exported to CSV.  The reactivity script then detected
state-change events (cold_to_hot, hot_to_cold, migration) per page, aligned all
instances at t=0, and scored each signal by:

  amplitude   -- how far the signal moves around the event (z-score units)
  consistency -- fraction of individual events that move in the same direction
  lead_lag_ms -- when the move starts relative to the transition (negative = LEADS)
  score       -- amplitude * consistency  (the primary ranking metric)

Three workloads produced complementary data:
  hot_cold   -- 10% hot (64 hits/sweep), 20% warm (8), 70% cold (1/37 pages)
  sequential -- uniform 4 hits/page/sweep across all pages
  temporal   -- phase 1: region A hot (3 s), phase 2: region B hot (3 s), A goes cold

================================================================================
SIGNAL GROUPS -- WHICH SIGNALS MEASURE THE SAME THING
================================================================================

Understanding the overlap is critical before building the phase-2 model.
Keeping redundant signals wastes model capacity and can mislead importance ranking.

GROUP 1: VOLATILITY / MAGNITUDE-OF-RATE-CHANGE
  [random_walk_index_high, system_quality_number, swing_index,
   accum_swing_index, random_walk_index_low]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All five measure the same underlying concept: how large a move the access rate
just made relative to its recent baseline.

  random_walk_index_high
      Random Walk Index (high period).  Compares actual range to the range
      expected from a random walk.  A value > 1 means a directional move.

  system_quality_number
      System Quality Number.  Expectancy / std of rate changes.  Measures
      trend quality; spikes when the rate moves monotonically.

  swing_index
      Swing Index.  Uses |close - prev_close| relative to True Range.
      Numerically different from the two above but fires on the same events.

  accum_swing_index
      Accumulation Swing Index.  Cumulative sum of swing_index.  Because each
      bar adds to the running total, it is just swing_index with memory.

  random_walk_index_low
      Random Walk Index computed over the low period (shorter lookback).
      Consistently weaker than random_walk_index_high (~60-80% of its score)
      because the shorter window catches less of the sustained move.

  >>> random_walk_index_high and system_quality_number are nearly
      interchangeable.  swing_index and accum_swing_index are nearly
      interchangeable (the latter is the cumulative sum of the former).
      Pick ONE from this group.  random_walk_index_high is recommended:
      highest score, most consistent across workloads.

GROUP 2: TREND/CYCLE STATE  [schaff_trend_cycle, schaff_trend_cycle_signal]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These two are the same indicator at different smoothing stages.

  schaff_trend_cycle
      Schaff Trend Cycle raw output.

  schaff_trend_cycle_signal
      Signal line of the Schaff Trend Cycle (another EMA pass over it).

  Both consistently LAG transitions by +50 to +100 ms (they confirm after the
  fact, they do not predict).  They are useful as confirmation signals, not
  as features for early prediction.

  >>> The two are redundant; schaff_trend_cycle_signal adds one more EMA lag
      on top of schaff_trend_cycle.  If you include this group, use
      schaff_trend_cycle only.

GROUP 3: BINARY DIRECTION FLAGS  [parabolic_sar_direction, supertrend_direction]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both output +1 (uptrend) or -1 (downtrend).

  parabolic_sar_direction
      Direction flip of the Parabolic SAR.

  supertrend_direction
      Direction flip of the Supertrend band.

  Both react at hot->cold transitions (score ~0.40-1.11) and are coincident
  (lead_lag ~0 ms).  supertrend_direction is consistently weaker than
  parabolic_sar_direction.

  >>> Keep parabolic_sar_direction, drop supertrend_direction.

GROUP 4: DIRECTIONAL MOVEMENT
  [plus_directional_indicator, minus_directional_indicator,
   avg_directional_index]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All three come from the same Directional Movement calculation.

  plus_directional_indicator
      Positive DI: upward directional momentum.

  minus_directional_indicator
      Negative DI: downward directional momentum.

  avg_directional_index
      Average of |+DI - -DI| / (+DI + -DI) over the lookback period.

  minus_directional_indicator is the most useful of the three for hot->cold
  detection (score 0.40-1.43 across workloads).  avg_directional_index and
  plus_directional_indicator are weaker and noisier.

  >>> If you need a direction signal from this family, use
      minus_directional_indicator only.

GROUP 5: AROON FAMILY  [aroon_up, aroon_down, aroon_oscillator]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
aroon_oscillator = aroon_up - aroon_down.  It is algebraically derived from the
other two, so all three carry identical information.

  aroon_up
      Time since the highest rate in the lookback window.

  aroon_down
      Time since the lowest rate in the lookback window.

  aroon_oscillator
      Their difference.

  Moderate reactivity (score ~0.10-2.40 on temporal).  aroon_oscillator is the
  most compact summary of the three.

  >>> Keep aroon_oscillator, drop aroon_up and aroon_down.

GROUP 6: INTER-ACCESS TIMING
  [inter_access_interval_ms, inter_access_variance_ms2]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both are derived from the distribution of gaps between consecutive accesses.

  inter_access_interval_ms
      Mean gap between accesses in the bar window.

  inter_access_variance_ms2
      Variance of those gaps.

  inter_access_interval_ms is the ONLY signal that reliably LEADS cold_to_hot:
  lead_lag = -100 ms to -250 ms across workloads.  Before a page heats up,
  access gaps begin tightening -- the interval signal catches this early.
  Consistency is moderate (0.53-0.72) so it is noisier than the volatility
  family, but its predictive lead makes it uniquely valuable for phase 2.

  inter_access_variance_ms2 is a weaker, noisier companion.  It occasionally
  leads cold_to_hot but with lower consistency.

  >>> Keep inter_access_interval_ms.  inter_access_variance_ms2 is optional
      (adds noise, minor incremental signal over the mean).

GROUP 7: ICHIMOKU CLOUD
  [ichimoku_tenkan, ichimoku_kijun, ichimoku_senkou_a, ichimoku_senkou_b,
   ichimoku_chikou]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All five are moving-average-type smoothings of the access rate over different
lookback windows (9, 26, 52 bars).  They are far too slow to respond to the
transitions in this data (6-second workloads with 50 ms bars).

  Additionally: ichimoku_chikou produces IDENTICAL output to
  interval_access_rate in every workload (same amplitude, same score, same
  lead_lag).  This is a code-level issue -- the chikou line appears to be
  replicated from the raw rate.

  Scores across all workloads and events: amplitude < 0.42, score < 0.40.
  In most configurations they are essentially noise (score < 0.05).

  >>> DROP ENTIRE GROUP.  No predictive or reactive value in this dataset.

GROUP 8: LINEAR REGRESSION  [linear_reg_slope, linear_reg_intercept]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both are near-zero across all workloads and events (score < 0.03 in most cases).
The regression window is too long for 50 ms transitions.

  >>> DROP BOTH.  Not reactive to state changes at this timescale.

GROUP 9: TREND-VS-CHOP FILTERS
  [vertical_horizontal_filter, gopalakrishnan_range_index]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both measure the ratio of net price movement to total path length (trend vs.
oscillation detection).

  vertical_horizontal_filter
      Vertical Horizontal Filter.  Moderate reactivity (score 0.37-0.58 on
      hot_to_cold), consistent (~0.80), coincident.

  gopalakrishnan_range_index
      Gap Oscillator.  Slightly weaker than vertical_horizontal_filter in
      most workloads.

  These measure the same concept from slightly different angles.
  vertical_horizontal_filter is the stronger performer.

  >>> Keep vertical_horizontal_filter, drop gopalakrishnan_range_index if
      reducing feature count.

GROUP 10: MISC INDIVIDUAL SIGNALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  triple_exp_rate_of_change
      Triple-smoothed rate of change.  HIGH amplitude (up to 5.6 on temporal
      cold_to_hot) but extremely LOW consistency (0.06-0.18).  Fires loudly
      but randomly.  Not reliable.  DROP.

  range_action_verification_index
      Relative vigor; moderate reactivity.  Lags transitions.  Optional, no
      unique predictive contribution.

  heat_score
      Composite of UFFD + PEBS counts.  Moderate reactivity (score
      0.10-1.57), coincident.  Useful as a secondary confirmation feature
      but does not lead transitions.

  recency_weighted_frequency
      Near-zero reactivity across all workloads (score < 0.01).  DROP.

  access_rate / interval_access_rate
      Raw UFFD-based rate.  Near-zero reactivity (score < 0.007).  PEBS-based
      signals outperform this by orders of magnitude.  DROP as standalone
      features; they are already embedded in the signal computations.

  parabolic_sar
      The raw Parabolic SAR value (not direction).  Negligible reactivity.
      Use parabolic_sar_direction instead.

  supertrend
      Raw Supertrend band value.  Negligible reactivity.  Use
      parabolic_sar_direction or supertrend_direction instead (and prefer
      parabolic_sar_direction).

SUMMARY: RECOMMENDED FEATURE SET FOR PHASE-2 MODEL
================================================================================

Based on cross-workload consistency, lead timing, and inter-signal redundancy:

  KEEP (non-redundant, reactive, at least partially leading):

    random_walk_index_high
        Top volatility signal.  Coincident.  Highest and most consistent
        score in all 3 workloads.  Represents the entire GROUP 1 family.

    schaff_trend_cycle
        Trend/cycle state.  Lags +50-100 ms but has high consistency.  The
        only signal in its group with meaningful reactivity.  Use as a
        confirmation feature.

    inter_access_interval_ms
        ONLY signal that leads cold_to_hot (-100 to -250 ms).  Noisier than
        the volatility family but provides timing information no other
        signal has.  Critical for the prediction task.

    parabolic_sar_direction
        Binary direction flag.  Coincident.  Low-cost feature for the model
        to learn transition direction.

    minus_directional_indicator
        Directional indicator.  Hot_to_cold detection.  Complements
        parabolic_sar_direction with a continuous value.

    vertical_horizontal_filter
        Trend-vs-oscillation filter.  Moderate reactivity, high consistency.
        Different axis from random_walk_index_high.

  OPTIONAL (adds marginal signal, not redundant):

    heat_score
        Composite UFFD+PEBS count.  Non-zero contribution in the sequential
        workload.  Low effort to include.

    aroon_oscillator
        Time-since-high/low.  Moderate reactivity.  Covers a different angle

================================================================================
HONEST CAVEATS (for mentor discussion)
================================================================================

1. SYNTHETIC WORKLOADS ONLY
   All three workloads were purpose-built to produce graded intensity and clean
   transitions.  Real application memory patterns are messier and may surface
   different signal rankings.  This is phase-1 discovery; phase-2 should validate
   on a real workload trace (e.g., Redis, SPEC, or a research benchmark).

2. RESIDUAL PEBS THROTTLE DIPS
   At sample period 5003, the kernel still produced occasional throttle bursts on
   heavily-hammered hot pages.  These cause 1-2 bar dips in access_rate that are
   visible in raw data.  The median smoothing in the event detector suppresses
   false event detection from these dips, but they add low-level noise to signal
   values computed over bars containing dips.  This is cosmetic: hot pages never
   reach zero rate during the active phase.

3. SINGLE RUN PER WORKLOAD
   Each workload was run once.  Signal rankings are stable enough to present as
   findings, but replication (2-3 runs, compare ranks) would strengthen the case.
   The top-3 ranking (random_walk_index_high / system_quality_number /
   swing_index) is unlikely to change; lower-ranked signals may swap positions
   across runs.

4. GROUP 1 IS NEAR-DEGENERATE
   random_walk_index_high, system_quality_number, and swing_index all measure the
   same thing (magnitude of rate change).  Their high scores reflect the fact that
   rate changes ARE the transitions -- these signals are reactive by construction.
   The interesting finding is whether any signal leads the transition, which is
   where inter_access_interval_ms stands out as genuinely predictive rather than
   merely reactive.

5. MIGRATION EVENT RELIABILITY
   Migration event counts are small (92-267 per workload) compared to cold_to_hot
   and hot_to_cold (1000-9000+).  Consistency scores of 1.0 on migration events
   should be treated skeptically -- small n inflates consistency estimates.
